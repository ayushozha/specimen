# Specimen launch film: three storylines

The one thing: **Specimen captures any website exactly, section by section, and hands it to the tool you build in.**
Remember: "I can take that exact section, colours and motion included, straight into Figma, Framer or my coding agent."
Spine: one designer, one real section with a pointer effect (the ink hero), carried from the live page to the finished output.

Formats for all three: 16:9 master at 1920×1080, 30 fps, 40–45 s; a 9:16 cut at 1080×1920, 28–32 s, from the same timeline.
Sound for all three: a real music bed, recorded UI sounds on visible actions only, and a natural voiceover.

---

## A. One section, three tools (recommended)

**Device:** one object across apps. **Form:** object-to-object chain.
Recommended because a stranger follows one thing from start to finish, and the payoff is real output, not effects.

A designer is on a site whose hero has an ink trail that follows the cursor. Specimen's four capture corners snap around the hero, the real panel captures it with the live preview, and the captured section becomes the next world each time: editable Figma layers with the clip playing, a Framer component running the page's own shader under the pointer, and a DESIGN.md that Claude Code builds from.

- **First 2 s:** the cursor drags an ink trail across a real hero. The four white corners close around it. Hook text: "Capture any website. Exactly."
- **Beats:** hook (ink hero, corners) → tension (a flat screenshot, an eyedropper guessing a hex, "what easing is that?") → reveal "Specimen" in the side panel → proof: choose, capture (live preview filling in), use it → payoff: the same hero alive in Figma, Framer and the agent, then collapsing back into one frame → claim → end card (the corners become the logo, the install line).
- **Look:** the website's own world. Warm paper `#fffaf5`, indigo ink `#2b2849`, Manrope, the banner's pink/lavender/blue as the one material, with the night indigo `#211d3d` for the motion world. The accent is the iris gradient, used only on captured things.
- **Sound:** a warm, minimal pulse around 110 bpm that holds back during the tension, opens when the panel appears, and hits once on the payoff. Clicks on real clicks, a soft shutter for each capture, whooshes on world changes (2–3 takes rotated).

## B. Guessing vs knowing

**Device:** life before and after. **Form:** split screen to one.

The left half is the old way, churning: a screenshot, an eyedropper, a stopwatch on an animation, a pasted PNG that won't edit. The right half is Specimen doing the same job calmly. The halves race until the left gives up and the right fills the frame with exact values: `oklch(0.62 0.19 255)`, `cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1)`, the shader's own code.

- **First 2 s:** split frame. Left: the eyedropper misses by one shade. Right: the exact `oklch()` value lands. Text: "Stop guessing. Capture it."
- **Beats:** hook → the race (colours, motion, layers) → the left side gives up → the right takes the frame → payoff: one capture pours into Figma, Framer and an agent at once → claim → end card.
- **Look:** a grey, desaturated old world against the paper-and-iris new world; Manrope and a mono face for exact values.
- **Sound:** a ticking, tense bed under the old side; the music resolves to major when the split closes.

## C. Every value, exactly (the bold one)

**Device:** type carries the argument. **Form:** cut on the beat.

A kinetic type film. Each word is captured inside Specimen's corners as it lands: "every section", "every colour", "every curve", "every shader". Between the words, half-second inserts of the real panel prove each one. It plays like a title sequence.

- **First 2 s:** the corners snap shut on the word "Exactly." in black type on paper, on the downbeat.
- **Beats:** hook → four claims, each a word and its proof insert, cut on the half beat → payoff: all the inserts tile into the full panel → claim → end card.
- **Look:** big Manrope on paper and night indigo, alternating on the bar; the iris gradient only inside the corners.
- **Sound:** a driving 124 bpm track with a drop on the tiling; the voice speaks only the claim and the CTA.

---

What decides it: A shows the whole product doing one real job (the strongest launch). B argues against the old way. C is the most striking, but explains least.
